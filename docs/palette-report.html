Palette evaluation for agent CLIs

Measured WCAG 2.1 + APCA 0.1.9 across 21 palettes, canonical upstream hexes, generated by scripts/palette-report.py.

Long-session view (the question): light → modus-operandi (86.0), dark → catppuccin-mocha (58.8). Both weightings pick the same two winners, so the winner is not an artefact of the weights.

What the weighting changes is the order behind them. The biggest reorder is everforest-light-soft, +14.9 (42.7 long-session vs 27.8 contrast-first). Colour is worth .20 here and .00 in the compliance view, so the most colourful palette measured — catppuccin-latte at 91/100 — climbs, and still cannot reach the top because contrast carries .55 of the weight on its own.

The dark winner trades bloom for contrast, and that is a real cost. catppuccin-mocha holds 11.34:1 body text and 7.08:1 at its weakest accent, but its peak-foreground span is 0.7623 — the widest among dark contenders. everforest-dark-med-fixed is the bloom pick at 0.5372 span and would win if you weight halation hard. modus-operandi needs no repair at all; catppuccin-mocha needs none either.

Eye strain is not a property of a palette. Contrast and brightness are proxies for less decoding effort; colour is a preference plus a syntax-separation aid, not a strain lever. The fatigue literature found no significant polarity effect; polarity is set by the room.

Leaderboard

Two weightings, one set of measurements. Long-session is the ranking used below: body .15, dim .15, worst accent .15, accent coverage .10, colour .20, comfort .15, repair .10. Contrast-first is the earlier compliance view: body .20, dim .20, worst accent .20, coverage .15, comfort .10, repair .15, colour .00. The delta column is long-session minus contrast-first, so a positive number means the palette gains when colour and comfort are counted.

Two standards gate every accent term. worst accent and accents ≥4.5:1 are WCAG 2.1; accents Lc≥60 and worst Lc are APCA 0.1.9, where 60 is the content-text floor. An accent counts toward coverage only when it clears both, and the worst-accent axis takes the weaker of the two scaled to its own pass mark — a 12:1 accent that blooms cannot saturate it. The two standards agree in light mode on this set; they part company in dark mode, where APCA demotes the soft palettes that WCAG passes.

Spider graph

Seven axes, each 0–100 and higher-is-better, computed from the tables below: body-text ratio, dim-text contrast, worst accent, share of accents clearing both gates (WCAG 4.5:1 and APCA Lc 60), colour (mean Oklab chroma plus worst accent-pair ΔE), comfort headroom against the mode's failure mode (glare in light, halation in dark), and repairability (identity cost). The same seven values feed both weightings; only the weights differ, so the shape is the evidence and the score is a reading of it. Hover or tab a legend row to fade every other series. Derived variants marked shipped are not plotted: their shape equals their source by construction, so the leaderboard carries their delta instead. A palette can lose on the mean and still be the right pick for a mood. Reference palettes (primer-light, primer-dark) are audited UI systems rather than syntax themes, so they stay unranked and unplotted: their scores appear in the leaderboards, but their shapes would set the scale's extremes instead of being compared.

Terminal preview

Each pane renders the palette's own colours on its own background over a mock agent-CLI transcript: prompt, search result, a diff pair, a warning, an error and an agent label. The eight canonical ANSI roles are the palette's published accents — magenta falls back to purple/mauve, cyan to aqua/teal, black to the dim colour, white to body text. The faded second row is a derived bright variant (same hue and saturation, lightness moved 10% away from the background), because these projects publish 6–9 accents rather than a 16-colour ANSI table: set your terminal from the canonical values and treat the brights as illustrative. Bloom and glare are not simulated. Halation depends on your room, your pupil and your display luminance; a screenshot cannot reproduce it. Read the span column instead.

Method, and what this does not measure

  • WCAG 2.1 relative luminance, ratio (L1+.05)/(L2+.05). Polarity-blind: white-on-black and black-on-white score identically, so it cannot see halation.
  • APCA 0.1.9 (W3C silver draft constants). Polarity-aware, signed: negative Lc means light text on dark. Content floor |Lc| ≥ 60, body floor 75, and |Lc| > 95 in dark mode is the halation ceiling, not a win. APCA 0.2.x uses different exponents — these numbers are version-specific.
  • Colour is two things measured in Oklab: mean chroma across accents (hedonic — how colourful it looks), and the worst pairwise Oklab distance between two accents (functional — whether a string and a keyword remain distinguishable). Chroma maps 0.08–0.20 to 0–100 and separability maps 0.04–0.06 to 0–100, then 0.6/0.4. Those bands are observed ranges over these twelve palettes, not perceptual thresholds: the axis ranks the set, it does not certify a palette.
  • Repair cost holds hue and saturation constant and moves lightness until the pair clears 4.5:1, in 0.004 HLS lightness steps toward the far pole. Cost is ΔL × 100, in lightness percentage points, not a percentage change or a perceptual distance. Only failing accents enter the repair table; body and dim failures remain visible above.
  • Peak-foreground span is max(relLum) − relLum(bg) over body, dim and accent roles. The peak can be an accent, but body brightness is the primary sustained halation lever. Light-mode spans are negative by construction, so those leaderboards show surface relative luminance instead.
  • Composite and radar: body = min(100, ratio / 21 × 100); light dim = min(100, ratio / 7 × 100); dark dim = min(100, |Lc| / 75 × 100); worst accent = min(min(100, ratio / 4.5 × 100), min(100, |Lc| / 60 × 100)); accent coverage = accents clearing both gates / total × 100. Light comfort = clamp((1 − surface relLum) / 0.12 × 100, 0, 100); dark comfort = clamp((1 − peak span / 0.90) × 100, 0, 100). Repairability = max(0, 100 − max repair / 25 × 100). These are declared scoring heuristics, not validated comfort scales; a high score does not override a dark-mode |Lc| > 95 warning. Scores compare palettes within a mode.
  • Precision and bands: ratios use four decimals and signed Lc uses two; thresholds use unrounded values. AAA ≥ 7, AA ≥ 4.5, FAIL < 4.5 apply the WCAG normal-text thresholds. APCA floors here are screening heuristics; font size and weight still matter. Role hexes are embedded snapshots, not measurements of installed ports.
  • Two standards, not one: WCAG 2.1 is polarity-symmetric and saturates at 4.5:1, so it can see neither halation above the dark ceiling nor how far a saturated accent falls below APCA's content floor. APCA 0.1.9 models polarity but is a draft, its thresholds are guidance rather than a standard, and it weights luminance only — a vivid accent can pass WCAG and sit at Lc 40. Rather than average the two (which would invent weights) each accent term takes the weaker standard, so a palette has to satisfy both to score well, and the tables show both counts so the disagreement stays visible.
  • Not measured: font size and weight, room brightness, monitor luminance, blink rate, astigmatism, or how long you actually look away. These limit what this comparison can predict. The cited polarity studies favor positive polarity for decoding tasks, especially small text at night; that does not establish that a palette reduces eye strain.
